# Speaking at PMA's AI Product Marketing Summit

In August 2026 I gave a session at Product Marketing Alliance's AI Product Marketing Summit. 1,250+ product marketers. A live demo. No safety net.

The talk was called something like "How to build a marketing brain" - the exact working idea was that most PMMs use AI to do existing tasks faster, and the better question is how to make the AI smarter about your specific product, customer, and market so the outputs are actually good rather than just quick.

---

## What I was trying to show

The demo had one job: show a PMM thinking clearly in real time, not staging a result.

I built a private context system - competitors, customer language, voice standards, product framing - around a real marketing use case. Then I opened five prompts on camera. The system did a competitive ad teardown, drafted a LinkedIn ad using only sourced claim and customer language, I gave three pieces of feedback out loud, and the system incorporated them as standing rules. Not for this session. For every future session.

The point: the tool got smarter from the feedback loop. That is what the marketing brain concept is about. Not just faster. Actually better over time.

---

## How I built it

I spent time before the session making the on-camera output impossible to fake. The context files that feed the system contain real competitor data, real customer language, and explicit voice rules - so when I prompted it live, there was no planted answer to land on. The correct output emerged from the quality of the system, not from rehearsing which prompts to type.

There is a specific detail here worth keeping: I built a mechanism that hides any rehearsal outputs before a live recording. The system can produce ten test runs before I go on camera, and none of them are visible during the actual session. So the audience sees the system work from scratch, but they are not watching a first-ever attempt that might fail. That is the right way to demonstrate a live system.

The teleprompter was a custom build on an Elgato display: mid-grey text to reduce glare, voice-follow so the text moves at speaking pace rather than a fixed clock, with manual scroll as the fallback. The QR cards at the end of the session pointed at a real page where attendees could download the starter pack and start building their own version.

---

## What happened

1,250+ people in the room. The demo ran first take. Three prompts in, the system surfaced competitive data from a teardown I had run the week before. The ad it drafted was genuinely good - specific claim, sourced from real customer language, ended on the free offer rather than the paid ask. The three-things feedback I gave on camera (attribution, no price, end on free) became standing rules the system would apply to every ad going forward.

108 people from the session subscribed to Intelligent Growth, the newsletter I run where I write about exactly this kind of system.

---

## What this shows about how I operate

Most people treat AI as a typing assistant. I treat it as an operating system for the thinking work that PMMs do. Building the brain before the talk, making the demo unfakeable, structuring the feedback loop so the system compounds - none of that is technically difficult. It is just being deliberate about what you are building and what it needs to do.

The practical version: if I build a marketing brain at the start of a role and keep feeding it good inputs, by month 6 I am operating with context that would take a new hire months to acquire. That is the leverage case, and the Summit was me demonstrating it in public rather than just claiming it in an interview.
